abstract: Strategyproof mechanisms provide robust equilibrium with minimal assumptions
  about knowledge and rationality but can be unachievable in combination with other
  desirable properties such as budget-balance, stability against deviations by coalitions,
  and computational tractability. In the search for maximally-strategyproof mechanisms
  that simultaneously satisfy other desirable properties, we introduce a new metric
  to quantify the strategyproofness of a mechanism, based on comparing the payoff
  distribution, given truthful reports, against that of a strategyproof "reference"
  mechanism that solves a problem relaxation. Focusing on combinatorial exchanges,
  we demonstrate that the metric is informative about the eventual equilibrium, where
  simple regretbased metrics are not, and can be used for online selection of an effective
  mechanism.
