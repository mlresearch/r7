abstract: Many applications of causal analysis call for assessing, retrospectively,
  the effect of withholding an action that has in fact been implemented. This counterfactual
  quantity, sometimes called "effect of treatment on the treated," (ETT) have been
  used to to evaluate educational programs, critic public policies, and justify individual
  decision making. In this paper we explore the conditions under which ETT can be
  estimated from (i.e., identified in) experimental and/or observational studies.
  We show that, when the action invokes a singleton variable, the conditions for ETT
  identification have simple characterizations in terms of causal diagrams. We further
  give a graphical characterization of the conditions under which the effects of multiple
  treatments on the treated can be identified, as well as ways in which the ETT estimand
  can be constructed from both interventional and observational distributions.
