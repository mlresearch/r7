abstract: Products of Hidden Markov Models (PoHMMs) are an interesting class of generative
  models which have received little attention since their introduction. This may be
  in part due to their more computationally expensive gradient-based learning algorithm,
  and the intractability of computing the log likelihood of sequences under the model.
  In this paper, we demonstrate how the partition function can be estimated reliably
  via Annealed Importance Sampling. We perform experiments using contrastive divergence
  learning on rainfall data and data captured from pairs of people dancing. Our results
  suggest that advances in learning and evaluation for undirected graphical models
  and recent increases in available computing power make PoHMMs worth considering
  for complex time-series modeling tasks.
